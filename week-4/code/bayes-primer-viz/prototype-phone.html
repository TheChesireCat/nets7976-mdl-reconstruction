The whole Bayesian workflow, on something small

Young, Cantwell and Newman fit a model, sample its posterior with Stan, check the sampler and then check the model. Each step is standard, but in the paper they arrive all at once. This page takes them one at a time, first on Laplace's birth counts and then on a pile of counts drawn from two Poissons.

1Bayes on a grid

§1 multiply, normalize \(\;p(\theta) \to p(\theta \mid y)\) exact§2 draw, averageThe route

Laplace asked whether a birth is more likely to be a boy.Carpenter §5. The counts are Laplace's live births in Paris, 1745 to 1770. Let \(\theta\) be that probability. The data are \(y\) boys out of \(n\) births, so the likelihood is binomial. With a \(\Beta(a, b)\) prior the posterior is again a Beta,This is what conjugate means: prior and posterior come from the same family. which gives an exact answer to check everything else against:

\[ p(\theta \mid y) \;\propto\; \underbrace{\theta^{a-1} (1-\theta)^{b-1}}_{\text{prior}} \cdot \underbrace{\theta^{y} (1-\theta)^{n-y}}_{\text{likelihood}} \;=\; \Beta(a + y,\ b + n - y) \]
\(\displaystyle p(\theta \mid y) \;\propto\; \theta^{a-1} (1-\theta)^{b-1} \cdot \theta^{y} (1-\theta)^{n-y}\)
prior × likelihood
\(\displaystyle \phantom{p(\theta \mid y)} = \Beta(a + y,\ b + n - y)\)
again a Beta

With one parameter you can also skip the algebra: evaluate prior × likelihood on a fine grid of \(\theta\) values and divide by the sum. Figure 1 does both, and the two agree.

Data overwhelm the prior. With 0.01% of the data, about 20 births, the prior sliders move the posterior a lot. With all of it, no reasonable prior matters: \(P(\theta \le \tfrac12)\) drops to about \(10^{-42}\). That is Laplace's answer.

The grid works because there is only one parameter. With \(d\) parameters a grid needs \(400^d\) points, which is why the rest of the page uses samples.

3The toy problem: friends and strangers

§2 draw, average§3 add a hidden label \(\;z_i \to x_i \mid z_i\) the model§4 walk a chainThe route

§2, Monte Carlo, is left out of the prototype.

You have \(N\) counts, each one how often a pair of animals was seen together.This is the dolphin model of Young et al. §3.1 with the network taken away. A pair is either strangers, who meet by chance about \(\lambda_0\) times, or friends, who meet about \(\lambda_1\) times. A share \(\rho\) of the pairs are friends. The hidden label \(z_i\) says which kind pair \(i\) is:

\[ \begin{aligned} z_i &\sim \Bernoulli(\rho) && \text{is pair } i \text{ friends? (hidden)} \\ x_i \mid z_i &\sim \Poisson(\lambda_{z_i}) && \text{how often they were seen together} \end{aligned} \]
\(z_i \sim \Bernoulli(\rho)\)
is pair \(i\) friends? Hidden.
\(x_i \mid z_i \sim \Poisson(\lambda_{z_i})\)
how often they were seen together. Observed.

From the counts alone, find \(\theta = (\lambda_0, \lambda_1, \rho)\) and, for each pair, \(P(z_i = 1 \mid x)\). The strip above Figure 2 sets the true values used to simulate the counts. The samplers never see them, so you can check what they recover.

\(z\) is discrete, so the samplers never touch it. HMC needs a gradient, so §4 and §5 use the likelihood with \(z\) summed out and recover the \(z\)'s afterwards.

4When you can't draw directly: Metropolis vs. HMC

§3 add a hidden label§4 walk a chain \(\;\to\) correlated draws exact in the limit§5 sum out \(z\)The route

This section holds \(\rho\) at its true value, so the posterior of the §3 counts is a surface over \((\lambda_0, \lambda_1)\) that you can see. There is no formula for drawing from it, but a Markov chain can walk it so that its positions, in the long run, are draws from it.

The dolphin model is the toy model with pairs of animals in place of counts. Each pair \((i, j)\) has a count \(X_{ij}\) and a hidden edge \(A_{ij}\), and the three pieces are:

\[ \begin{aligned} &\text{data model} \quad && \mu_{ij}(1) = \Poisson(X_{ij} \mid \lambda_1) \quad && \mu_{ij}(0) = \Poisson(X_{ij} \mid \lambda_0) \quad && \lambda_1 > \lambda_0 \quad \text{(breaks the symmetry)} \\ &\text{network model} \quad && \nu_{ij}(1) = \rho && \nu_{ij}(0) = 1 - \rho \\ &\text{prior on } \theta \quad && \rho \sim \Uniform(0, 1) && \lambda_k \sim \Normal^{+}(1, 100) && \text{(the authors' code; the text says half-normal, } \sigma = 100\text{)} \end{aligned} \]
