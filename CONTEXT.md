# NETS 7976 course site

Interactive companions to the readings of a directed study on MDL, network reconstruction and
message passing, written to be learned from like a textbook.

## Language

**Article**:
One week's walkthrough on a single page, following an argument step by step: either one paper's, or a
topic the papers assume (the Bayesian workflow primer is an article of the second kind).
_Avoid_: walkthrough page, viz, app, page (when the article is meant)

**Concept page**:
A wiki-style page for one idea the course leans on (e.g. expectation–maximization, Markov chain Monte Carlo),
written by us and linked from every article that uses it. One page covers a family of methods, with a section
for each member (Metropolis and HMC are sections of the MCMC page, not pages of their own).
_Avoid_: topic page, subtopic, wiki page

**Stub**:
A concept page that so far has only its lede, prerequisites and backlinks; its other sections are still to be written.

**Lede**:
The opening two or three sentences of a concept page or article, which define it and which its hover preview shows.
_Avoid_: summary, abstract, intro

**Backlink**:
A link on a concept page to each article section that uses the concept, listed under "Used in the course".

**External reference**:
A link to an outside explainer, usually Wikipedia, for basics that do not need a concept page of our own.
_Avoid_: basics link

**Prerequisite**:
A concept an article or concept page assumes the reader already has, stated up front and linked.

**Hover preview**:
A card that shows a summary of a linked concept page, external reference or article when the reader
hovers the link, in the manner of Gwern's popups and Wikipedia's page previews.
_Avoid_: tooltip (reserved for chart hover readouts), popup

**Reader**:
The person an article is written for: a first-year graduate student from another field, with calculus,
linear algebra and introductory probability.
